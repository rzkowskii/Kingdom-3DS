# Deviations from the original

Intentional differences from Kingdom: New Lands, with the reason for each.
Temporary stand-ins that will be replaced are marked **(temporary)**.

## Simulation

- **RNG.** `UnityEngine.Random` is replaced by a seeded xorshift32 (`game::rng`), which makes tests deterministic. The distributions match the original; the exact sequences don't.
- **Coin physics.** Unity uses Box2D. `game::coins` uses a small stand-in with the original values: gravity 15, coin bounciness 0.5 (Box2D takes the larger of the two surfaces' restitution), 1 m/s bounce threshold, and friction √(0.4 × 0.4) × g. Coins don't rotate or collide with each other. They should look and feel the same, but trajectories won't match frame-for-frame.
- **Monarch braking.** `Mover` never sets velocity downward; Box2D friction slows the monarch. We decelerate at √(1.0 × 0.4) × 15 ≈ 9.49 units/s², derived from the Player and default physics materials.
- **Character movement.** `Mover` is ported, but citizens don't have rigid bodies: they slow at a flat 6 units/s² (default friction), and the island edges clamp them instead of colliders.
- **Ground limits.** Farmland and grass never spread past the ends of the ground. The original's checks (an overlap test, and a bounds test that can never fail) rely on the beach and cliff colliders instead.
- **Forest edges at start.** The original fades out edge decorations over the first ~15 s. We remove them before the first frame. After a chop the delay is kept, but the 1 s fade isn't drawn.
- **Dead animals.** A dead deer or rabbit stays for 1 s for its death clip, then vanishes; the original pools it at the end of the clip.
- **Construction stand-ins.** The Castle6 keep builds itself in 10 s without its scaffold animation. The workshop's counters are separate objects at the workshop's sides.
- **Red moon.** The star field uses procedural positions and the PC sampling cadence. [Red-moon notes](p7-red-moon.md)
- **Bear swipes.** The bear attacks every 0.5 s while galloping; the original times it with an animation event in the gallop clip.
- **Inspired and grabbed citizens.** Inspiration skips the demotion entirely. A grabbed citizen is removed at once rather than carried visibly in the squid's hands **(temporary)**.
- **The boat (simplified).**
  - Stacked boat parts fall straight down and lie at their drop angle instead of tumbling under physics. A part a troll carries off is gone when the troll dies, as in the original, but it isn't dropped as debris.
  - Archers on the boat's guard slots move to the deck when it sails, instead of staying on the rails.
  - The dog is aboard at once; the original lets it run alongside for up to 8 s and then jump.
  - The crown isn't lost in the water past the beach (`TriggerCrownInWater`), and the invisible wall at the end of the jetty is the island edge.
  - No fish jump around the boat.
- **The map screen.** After a win the next island loads directly. The original's map lets you pick any unlocked island other than the current one, defaulting to the next. After a loss the same island starts again, as the map's Restart does.
- **Scenery is kept apart.** Purely decorative objects live in their own list (`Level::scenery`), which only drawing and forest edges look at. It keeps the per-tick scans short on an Old 3DS; behaviour is unchanged.
- **Saves are snapshots.**
  - The original stores each object's `IPersistent` data (`*Data` classes) and rebuilds the scene from prefabs. We serialize the whole simulation state, so a loaded game continues exactly, down to the RNG.
  - The cost is that saves aren't compatible across different `world.bin` builds or format versions; such saves are ignored.
  - There is a single slot (the original's `AUTO`), and no Continue/New Game menu yet: the desktop resumes the saved run unless started with `--new` or `--island`.
- **The purse's physics.** Box2D is replaced by a small solver in `game::bag`.
  - Each coin is three discs along its width instead of a hexagon.
  - Like Box2D, it uses sequential impulses with accumulated clamping, warm starting, speculative contacts and position correction. The bag's colliders are taken as-is.
  - Box2D sleeps whole islands of touching bodies. Here each coin sleeps once still (by speed, or by not having moved) and wakes when something lands on it.
  - How high coins pile, and so when they spill (about 55), is close but not exact.
- **The purse is always open.** On the 3DS bottom screen the bag stays open instead of fading in when coins change and closing 2–5 s later (`CoinBag.Show`/`HideLater`).
- **The map.**
  - It's drawn on the touch screen; the original shows it full screen between islands.
  - The figurines, clouds and pointer don't animate in (`mapicon_*`, `MapCloud.Reveal`), since the UI animation curves aren't exported. The boat and pointer cycle their frames at 4 fps.
  - Tapping the picked island again sets off, a touch shortcut for the action button.
- **Fixed timestep.** The simulation runs at 60 Hz (`state::TICK`). The original runs logic in `Update()` at a variable frame rate and physics at 50 Hz.

## Presentation

- **Recolor shader (reconstructed).** The `Custom/PowerSprite2` shader source isn't in the build. From the sprite data: alpha 176 marks pixels drawn in `_RecolorB` (skin / steed coat), and alpha 208 marks pixels drawn in `_RecolorA` (clothes / mane), each shaded by the pixel's red channel. Both are drawn opaque. The keyed pixels are colour-coded in the art (the monarch's cape yellow, the border's magenta and yellow, the castle shields' purple), and only their red channel follows the shading (1.0, 0.8, 0.67, 0.53), so multiplying the whole colour would tint the result.
- **Coin flight curve (temporary).** `Coin.moveCurve` is approximated with smoothstep until the extract tool exports AnimationCurves.
- **Camera (temporary).** A simple follow with velocity lead. `MainCamera`'s `leadOverTime` curve isn't ported yet.
- **Lighting (approximated).** `BlenderState` presets blend exactly as in `Blender`, but the shaders that consume them weren't exported. We multiply lit sprites and the fill and haze gradients (`Haze Material`, a lit `Custom/PowerSprite2`) by `ambientColor`, draw the sky gradient (`Custom/Sky`, no lighting inputs) in the presets' colours as they are, add `additiveColor` over the frame (`PostFX`), and draw the sky, fill and haze gradients at their scene depths. The finer shader effects (sparkle, wind sway, waves) aren't reproduced yet.
- **Moving parts (temporary).** Catapult arms, ballista bodies and the dog's tree don't rotate. The teleporter exit is drawn as placeholder rings. Wall damage doesn't show crumbling debris yet.
- **Sky clouds.** Both layers use the PC's tight meshes. Desktop evaluates the two shadow taps in the fragment shader. The 3DS evaluates exposure at native pixel centers separately for each stereo eye. [Stereo cloud shadow notes](p11-stereo-cloud-shadows.md)
- **Fog and mist.** Both weather layers use the PC controllers, depth, and tight sprite meshes. Fog takes warm light from nearby fires at its mesh vertices. The 3DS tests world Z from the first fog draw onward and writes depth at covered fog pixels. [Fog depth and fire timing notes](p14-fog-depth-and-fire-timing.md)
- **Precipitation (temporary).** Rain and snow still need their visual effects.
- **Stars (approximated).** The night field sits in the Heaven layer behind the sun and moon and fades with the blended `stars` level, as the original's `Stars` does, but its layout isn't exported: 110 fixed stars are placed procedurally, a sixth of them 2 px, each dipping gently in and out of brightness.
- **Celestial glows (approximated).** Each `CelestialBody` glow child is drawn with its own sprite, scale and `SpriteRenderer` colour, multiplied by the preset's glow colour, and fades out over the last stretch of its body's arc before it sets, so a set sun's wide halo can't paint the sky above the horizon after dark. The fade band's width (10% of the arc's height) is a guess.
- **Boat sail.** Before setting sail the sail shows its `Closed` clip with the emblem hidden; the emblem's look while furled is a guess.
- **Seasonal events.** `LevelConfig.seasonalGroupCounts` (Halloween/Christmas extras tied to the save's start date) are ignored.
- **Coat of arms (partly ported).** The kingdom's random banner colours (`CoatOfArms.Randomize()`) are ported: they colour the monarch's cape, tower soldiers and recoloured banner parts (primary/secondary). Banner patterns and emblems aren't drawn yet, and some banners swap primary and secondary by side **(temporary)**.
- **Water ripples by row.** The original's `Custom/Water` shader moves every pixel of the reflection by its two displacement maps (`water_coarse`, `water_fine`). The PICA200 has no fragment shaders, so each row of the reflection moves as a whole, by the maps sampled under the screen's centre. Like the original, it mirrors last frame's screen (on the 3DS, a GPU copy of the top screen's colour buffer), squashed by `reflectionScale`. The fine map follows camera movement and omits wind drift.
- **Sound format.** Clips are resampled to 22050 Hz and stored as DSP-ADPCM (the 3DS DSP's own format, so playback costs no CPU): effects in mono, the soundtrack in stereo. Effects are placed by a gentle pan (up to 80% toward a side at the sound's max distance) instead of Unity's 3D panning, whose listener hears a source fully left or right a step off centre (and, being rotated 180°, reversed).
- **Ambience mix (approximated).** The `AmbientManager`'s beds (forest day and night, meadow, crickets, wind, rain) follow their `TimeBasedAudio` hours and seasons, weighted by how far into a forest the camera is (`AmbientRegion`'s 8-unit edges) and by the rain level. Wind plays at the calm level. The port uses the wind simulation for sky clouds.
- **Not voiced yet.** Arrow impacts, deaths, the dog, chests, the teleporter, the boss and the cliff portal's rock sequence have sounds in the data but no game event to trigger them. Music doesn't cross-fade when the victory track interrupts a playing one (the old one stops).
- **Stereo 3D.** The original has no 3D. Each drawn item's depth comes from its distance behind the game layer, `z / (|z| + 10)`, and the top screen's far layers separate by up to 12 pixels at full slider; the game layer sits at the screen.
- **Night shelter.** At night peasants and workers stay inside the outermost *built, intact* wall on each side (the camp's minimum extent if there's none), and archers line up behind it. In the original peasants still stroll to outlying shops after dark, and the border (and so the archers' line) counts a wall still under construction. Farmers shelter at a farmhouse only if it's behind the walls. To match, after dark builders only take jobs at least 2 units inside those walls, and peasants only pick up tools behind them (anything further would send them out and straight back every tick); ballista crews still wind their ballistas wherever they stand, as in the original.
- **Fires (approximated).** Night-only fire starts and stops are staggered, torches follow the ambient threshold, and statue fires follow charge. The flame sprite and water streak appear before the lamp's delayed fade. The flame uses the nearest-sized `Flame` prefab's sprite animation, with the small animation covering particle-only flames. The port draws the water streak directly in the main scene and removes the flame sprite when emission stops. Game-layer sprites sample the light at their center, and the ground strip uses additive columns. Fog uses the PC's vertex lighting with the port's scene-local light list. [Fire timing notes](p14-fog-depth-and-fire-timing.md)
- **Night brightness.** Lit sprites get 7% of the way from the ambient light to full light, since handheld screens show the original's darkest nights as near black.
- **Water (approximated).** As `Custom/Water` (from the PC build's GLSL), the mirrored scene fades into `waterColor` with depth: `lerp(w × reflection + (1 − w) × ambient, waterColor, (v + 0.2) × waterColor.a)`, `w` = 1 − 0.8 × (rain + snow). How fast `v` grows below the shore (0.32 per unit) is fitted to the PC's midday water. Each row shifts as a whole rather than per pixel, and the shader's sparkles and ragged shoreline aren't drawn. The 3DS screen shows less water depth than the PC's 16:9 view, so its water reads a little more mirror-like at the bottom.
- **Save slots.** Three game slots instead of the original's one auto slot; autosaves go to the slot in use, and unlocks stay shared. The touch screen's pause menu saves to and loads from them (loading an empty slot starts a new game there).
- **Stamina meter.** The touch screen shows the mount's stamina under the daylight bar (red-orange while tired, gold while well fed); the original only shows it by the steed panting.
- **Sound levels.** Clips are stored peak-normalized (raised at most 16×) and then driven 4× (+12 dB) through a look-ahead peak limiter, with the gain back to the original level kept in each `.adp`. The 3DS DSP can't be relied on to amplify a voice past full volume, so the loudness is baked in. The 3DS plays at twice that driven level (each voice capped at full volume, with the DSP's soft clipping) so its small speakers can be heard; the desktop divides the drive back out, so it hears the original levels, slightly compressed.
- **Compass.** The touch screen points the way back to the campfire, with the distance, under the stamina bar; the original has none. It helps after resuming a game in the woods.
- **Tutorial (partly ported).** On the first island of a fresh game, until a tutorial has been finished once (kept with the unlocks, like `PrefsStore`'s `TutorialCompleted`), the previous monarch's ghost waits until the monarch comes near. It then leads the way to the campfire, then to the nearest beggar, and once two citizens are hired, to the nearest wall site, as `Ghost.ShowGoal` does. Once two citizens are hired and a wall stands it fades over 5 s and is gone. The original's hint glyphs, its "this way" beckon text, the payable gating and the bow/hammer steps aren't ported, so the ghost picks its goals from the island and not from the hints. The ghost is always the king. A game saved before the ghost was added carries on without it.
- **The title vanishes instead of crumbling.** The KINGDOM letters in the forest count as passed once the monarch comes within 10 units of them (`Title`'s scanner), and are removed from the island as soon as they're 14 units behind, which is off-screen. The original breaks them into debris that stays on the ground; that animation looked wrong at 3DS scale.
- **God mode (testing aid).** SELECT + R + Y on the 3DS (I on desktop) fills the purse to 20 coins that spending doesn't use up (refilling a spent purse kept the bag's coin physics busy, which lagged on the 3DS) and stops anything from hurting the monarch, so the crown can't be lost. It isn't saved, and "GOD MODE" shows on the touch screen while it's on. With the chord in use, SELECT toggles the debug overlay on release rather than on press.
- **Banner editor (not in the original).** The pause menu's BANNER button opens an editor on the touch screen: king or queen, the coat of arms' pattern (14) and emblem (21), and its main, trim and emblem colors from a 48-color picker. The original only rolls these at random each run (`Player.RandomAppearance`, `CoatOfArms.Randomize`) or takes a typed blazon (`Menu.OnButtonSetBlazon`). What's picked shows at once on the castle banners, the boat's sail, the border banner's icon, and the monarch's cape, and it's kept with the unlocks for every run after, and for saved games when they're loaded. Until something is picked, each run rolls a king or queen, a pattern and one of the 14 ordinary emblems, as the original does. Banners are recognised by their sprites (`banner_1_pattern_*`, `banner_1_emblem_*`, `border_banner_icon`) rather than from the prefabs' `Banner` components, so the extracted world data (and the save fingerprint) stays the same. The castle's cloth is always recoloured, as `Banner.AssignColorAndPattern` does. Until something is picked, games saved before this load as a king with the first pattern and emblem. The ghost is always the king.
